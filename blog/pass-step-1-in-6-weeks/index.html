How to Pass USMLE Step 1 in 6 Weeks: The Minimum Effective Dose

Not everyone gets 3 months of dedicated time. Here is a 42-day plan that cuts the fat and focuses only on what passes the exam.

USMLE300 Team2026-03-05

If your NBME just dropped two weeks before your exam, take a breath. If you only have 6 weeks of dedicated study time and everyone online seems to have had 12, this guide is for you.

Six weeks is tight but absolutely doable for Step 1 — especially now that it’s pass/fail. You don’t need to master every topic. You need to reliably score above 196 on practice exams, and that requires a ruthlessly focused plan.

The Core Principle

In 6 weeks, you cannot cover everything. You must accept that some low-yield topics will be left unreviewed. The goal is not perfection — it’s consistent passing scores on practice exams by Week 5.

Your Resource Stack (Keep It Minimal)

  • UWorld — your primary learning tool. Aim to complete 70–80% of the bank.
  • First Aid — reference only. Do not read cover to cover.
  • Pathoma Chapters 1–3 — general pathology. Highest yield per minute in all of USMLE prep.
  • Anki (AnKing, targeted) — unsuspend cards only for systems you’re actively covering. Cap at 60–90 min/day.

That’s it. No Sketchy, no Boards & Beyond, no Physeo unless you’ve already been using them. Adding a new resource at this stage costs more time than it saves.

Week-by-Week Breakdown

Weeks 1–2: Foundation Sprint

Cover the Big 5 systems: Cardiology, Pulmonology, Renal, GI, and Endocrine. These make up roughly 40% of the exam. Do 80–120 UWorld questions per day by system, with thorough review. Watch Pathoma Ch 1–3 during this phase.

End of Week 1: Take NBME 25 or 26 as a baseline. Don’t panic about the score — it’s your starting line.

Weeks 3–4: Systems Sweep + Volume Ramp

Cover remaining systems: Neuro, Heme/Onc, MSK, Repro, Psych. Increase to 120–160 UWorld questions per day. Switch to random timed mode.

Dedicate 2 hours per week to high-yield side topics: biochem pathways, biostatistics (learn the 2×2 table cold), and ethics.

End of Week 4: Take NBME 27 or 28. You should see meaningful improvement from baseline.

Week 5: Test Readiness

Continue UWorld in random mode. Focus review time exclusively on your 3 weakest systems based on UWorld performance data. Take NBME 29 or 30.

Decision point: If your NBME shows 210+ with a clear upward trend, you’re on track. If below 200, seriously consider pushing your exam by 2 weeks.

Week 6: Taper and Peak

Take UWSA2 on Day 1 of this week (best predictor). Take Free 120 mid-week. Light review only in the final 3 days — skim First Aid rapid review, do 1 block of UWorld max per day. Stop studying by 2pm the day before your exam. Sleep, exercise, eat well.

The Taper Matters

Many students try to cram in the last week out of anxiety. This almost always hurts performance. By Week 6, the knowledge is either in your brain or it isn’t. Your job now is sleep, confidence, and pacing strategy.

What to Do If Your Practice Scores Aren’t Improving

Flat scores usually mean one of three things: you’re doing questions without thoroughly reviewing them, you’re avoiding your weakest subjects, or you’re burned out and need more rest. It’s rarely a volume problem.

Track your UWorld percentage by organ system. After 100+ questions in a system, your percentage becomes a reliable signal. Systems below 50% need dedicated content review. Systems above 65% need only maintenance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 6 weeks enough to pass Step 1?

Yes, for most students who have completed their preclinical curriculum. The key is ruthless prioritization — focus on the highest-yield 80% of content and accept that some low-yield topics will be left unreviewed.

What if my baseline NBME is below 180?

A baseline below 180 means you likely need more than 6 weeks. Consider pushing your exam date to 8–10 weeks and spending the extra time on content review before jumping into heavy question practice.

Should I do a second pass of UWorld in 6 weeks?

Usually not. With 6 weeks, your priority is seeing as much of the bank as possible with thorough review. If you finish early (rare), redo only your incorrects and marked questions rather than a full second pass.
