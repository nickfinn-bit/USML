03 · Assessment

Frameworks, not fact dumps

You've heard "just do more questions" or "use this resource." The problem: without a framework to organize knowledge, everything blurs together on exam day.

Each USMLE300 bootcamp builds a ground-up mental skeleton for one subject — the core concepts, the decision trees, the patterns that actually repeat on the exam. Then you hang the details on it.

  • A.Not content overload. We focus on the 20% of material that drives 80% of questions.
  • B.Not passive watching. You answer questions, defend your reasoning, and teach back.
  • C.Not isolated facts. Every concept connects to a structure you can reason through — even on questions you've never seen.
Shock States → CO/SVR patterns → Hemodynamic profiles
↳ Distributive: high CO, low SVR (early sepsis: bounding pulses)
↳ Tamponade vs constrictive: y descent blunted vs prominent
Murmur Localization → Timing → Maneuvers
↳ Left sternal border systolic = HCM; diastolic = AR or PR
↳ HCM and MVP both decrease with squatting (↑ preload)
Post-MI Complications → Timeline → Presentation
↳ 3–7 d: free wall rupture, papillary muscle rupture (acute MR)
↳ 2+ wk: Dressler syndrome, mural thrombus

This is how every session is taught: a structure you can reason through on exam day, not a list to memorize.
